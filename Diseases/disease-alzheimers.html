Alzheimer's Disease

The most common form of dementia, a progressive neurological disease that destroys memory and thinking skills. It accounts for 60-80% of all dementia cases.

Symptoms

Alzheimer's affects over 55 million people worldwide. It is the 7th leading cause of death globally. Early diagnosis allows for better planning and access to treatments that can temporarily slow symptoms.

  • Memory loss disrupting daily life, forgetting recently learned information
  • Difficulty with problem-solving and planning familiar tasks
  • Confusion with time, dates, seasons, and passage of time
  • Vision and spatial difficulties, trouble judging distances
  • Withdrawing from social activities and hobbies
  • Changes in mood, personality, confusion, suspicion, depression
Causes & Risk Factors

Alzheimer's is caused by abnormal protein deposits in the brain that disrupt communication between nerve cells, leading to cell death and brain shrinkage.

  • Amyloid plaques, abnormal clumps of beta-amyloid protein between neurons
  • Tau tangles, twisted strands of tau protein inside neurons
  • APOE-e4 gene, major genetic risk factor (3-4x increased risk)
  • Age is the biggest risk factor, doubles every 5 years after 65
  • Head injuries and poor cardiovascular health increase risk
  • Women have higher lifetime risk (partly due to longer lifespan)
Prevention & Lifestyle
  • Stay mentally active, learning new skills, reading, puzzles
  • Regular physical exercise, improves blood flow to brain
  • Maintain good cardiovascular health, control blood pressure and diabetes
  • Maintain social connections and engage with community
  • Mediterranean and MIND diet associated with lower risk
  • Quality sleep, brain clears amyloid during deep sleep
Treatment
  • Cholinesterase inhibitors, Donepezil, Rivastigmine, Galantamine (slow decline)
  • Memantine, for moderate to severe cases
  • Lecanemab (Leqembi), new anti-amyloid therapy; slows early-stage decline
  • Cognitive stimulation therapy and structured routine
  • Caregiver support and respite care programs
  • Safe home modifications, fall prevention, wandering alarms
