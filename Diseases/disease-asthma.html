Asthma

A chronic lung condition that causes inflammation and narrowing of the airways, leading to episodes of wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, and coughing.

Symptoms

Asthma affects approximately 262 million people worldwide. With proper treatment and trigger avoidance, most people with asthma can lead active, normal lives. It can develop at any age.

  • Recurrent wheezing (whistling sound when breathing)
  • Shortness of breath and chest tightness
  • Persistent cough, especially at night and early morning
  • Difficulty sleeping due to breathing problems
  • Symptoms worse during exercise or cold air exposure
  • Symptoms worsened by allergens like pollen, dust, and mold
Causes

Asthma results from a combination of genetic and environmental factors that cause chronic airway inflammation and hyper-responsiveness.

  • Genetic predisposition, family history is a major risk factor
  • Allergic sensitization to pollen, dust mites, pet dander
  • Air pollution, cigarette smoke exposure
  • Respiratory infections in early childhood
  • Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction
  • Occupational exposures (chemicals, dust, fumes)
Prevention
  • Identify and avoid your personal asthma triggers
  • Keep indoor air clean, air purifiers, HEPA filters
  • Reduce exposure to dust mites, wash bedding weekly in hot water
  • Do not smoke and avoid secondhand smoke
  • Annual flu vaccine to reduce respiratory infection risk
  • Monitor peak flow regularly to detect worsening early
Treatment
  • Short-acting bronchodilators (SABA), Salbutamol inhaler for quick relief
  • Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), main controller medication
  • Long-acting bronchodilators (LABA) combined with ICS
  • Leukotriene receptor antagonists (Montelukast)
  • Biologics (e.g. Omalizumab) for severe allergic asthma
  • Have an asthma action plan agreed with your doctor
