Major Depression

A common and serious mental health disorder causing persistent sadness, loss of interest, and a range of emotional and physical symptoms that significantly affect daily functioning.

Symptoms

Depression is not just feeling sad, it is a medical condition that affects how you think, feel, and handle daily activities. It is the leading cause of disability worldwide, affecting over 280 million people.

  • Persistent sad, anxious, or empty mood most of the day
  • Loss of interest or pleasure in activities once enjoyed
  • Changes in appetite, significant weight loss or gain
  • Sleep disturbances, insomnia or oversleeping
  • Fatigue and decreased energy
  • Difficulty concentrating, remembering, and making decisions
Causes

Depression results from a complex interaction of biological, psychological, and social factors. No single cause explains all cases.

  • Neurochemical imbalance, low serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine
  • Genetic factors, family history increases risk 2-3x
  • Life events, loss, trauma, stress, relationship problems
  • Medical conditions, thyroid disease, chronic pain
  • Substance abuse, alcohol and drugs worsen depression
  • Hormonal changes, postpartum, menopause
Prevention
  • Maintain regular sleep schedule, wake and sleep at same time
  • Exercise 30 minutes daily, proven to reduce depressive symptoms
  • Stay socially connected, isolation worsens depression
  • Limit alcohol and avoid recreational drugs
  • Learn stress management and relaxation techniques
  • Seek therapy early, do not wait for symptoms to become severe
Treatment
  • Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), most proven therapy
  • Antidepressants: SSRIs (Sertraline, Fluoxetine), first-line
  • SNRIs (Venlafaxine) for depression with anxiety
  • Interpersonal therapy and behavioural activation
  • Exercise as adjunct treatment
  • Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) for severe treatment-resistant cases
