Diabetes Mellitus

A chronic metabolic disease characterized by elevated blood glucose levels resulting from defects in insulin production, insulin action, or both.

Symptoms

Diabetes affects over 500 million people worldwide. While it cannot be cured, it can be managed effectively with lifestyle changes, medication, and regular monitoring to prevent serious complications.

  • Frequent urination (polyuria) especially at night
  • Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
  • Unexplained weight loss (especially Type 1)
  • Blurred vision and slow-healing wounds
  • Frequent infections (skin, gum, urinary tract)
  • Tingling or numbness in hands and feet
Causes

Diabetes occurs when the pancreas does not produce enough insulin or the body cannot effectively use the insulin it produces.

  • Type 1, Autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing beta cells
  • Type 2, Insulin resistance combined with progressive beta cell failure
  • Gestational diabetes, during pregnancy due to hormonal changes
  • Genetic factors, obesity, sedentary lifestyle increase Type 2 risk
Prevention
  • Maintain a healthy weight, even 5-10% weight loss improves blood sugar
  • Eat a balanced diet low in refined carbohydrates and sugar
  • Exercise for at least 150 minutes per week
  • Get regular blood sugar screenings if overweight or over 45
  • Avoid smoking and limit alcohol consumption
  • Manage stress, stress hormones raise blood sugar
Treatment
  • Lifestyle changes: healthy diet, exercise, weight management
  • Metformin, first-line medication for Type 2 diabetes
  • Insulin therapy, essential for Type 1, used in advanced Type 2
  • SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists for Type 2
  • Regular HbA1c, blood pressure, and cholesterol monitoring
  • Foot care and annual eye exams to detect complications early
