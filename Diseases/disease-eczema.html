Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)

A chronic inflammatory skin condition causing itchy, dry, red, and cracked skin. It commonly starts in childhood but can affect people of all ages.

Symptoms

Eczema affects about 10-20% of children and 1-3% of adults worldwide. While it cannot be cured, most people can manage their symptoms effectively with proper skincare and trigger avoidance.

  • Intense itching, especially at night
  • Dry, cracked, scaly skin patches
  • Red to brownish-grey patches on hands, feet, ankles, wrists, neck
  • Small, raised bumps that may weep fluid and crust
  • Thickened, leathery skin from scratching (lichenification)
  • Raw, swollen skin from scratching

Causes

Eczema results from a combination of genetic and environmental factors that impair the skin barrier and dysregulate the immune response.

  • Gene mutations affecting the skin barrier protein filaggrin
  • Overactive immune response to environmental triggers
  • Family history of eczema, hay fever, or asthma
  • Environmental triggers — soap, detergent, pollen, pet dander
  • Stress and sweating can trigger flare-ups
  • Food allergies (in some cases) — eggs, peanuts, dairy
Prevention
  • Moisturise skin twice daily with fragrance-free emollient
  • Identify and avoid personal triggers
  • Use mild, fragrance-free soaps and detergents
  • Keep fingernails short to prevent skin damage from scratching
  • Wear loose, soft, breathable clothing
  • Keep indoor humidity moderate (45-55%)
Treatment
  • Topical corticosteroids — first-line treatment for flares
  • Topical calcineurin inhibitors (Tacrolimus) for sensitive areas
  • Dupilumab (Dupixent) — biologic for moderate-severe eczema
  • Antihistamines for itching (sedating at night)
  • Wet wrap therapy for severe flares
  • Antibiotics if skin is infected from scratching
