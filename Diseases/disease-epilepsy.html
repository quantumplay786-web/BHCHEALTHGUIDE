Epilepsy

A chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures caused by abnormal electrical activity in the brain.

Symptoms

Epilepsy affects over 50 million people worldwide. About 70% of people with epilepsy can become seizure-free with appropriate anti-epileptic medication. The type of seizure depends on which part of the brain is affected.

  • Focal (partial) seizures, abnormal movement or sensation in one body part
  • Tonic-clonic (grand mal) seizures, loss of consciousness, body stiffening, jerking
  • Absence seizures, brief staring spells, common in children
  • Temporary confusion or staring episode
  • Uncontrollable jerking movements of arms and legs
  • Aura, unusual sensations or feelings before a seizure
Causes & Risk Factors

Epilepsy results from abnormal electrical discharges in brain neurons. In many cases no cause is found (idiopathic epilepsy).

  • Genetic mutations, many forms of epilepsy have hereditary components
  • Brain injuries, stroke, trauma, tumors, infections
  • Developmental disorders, autism spectrum disorder, Down syndrome
  • Previous brain infection, meningitis, encephalitis
  • Prenatal brain damage, lack of oxygen, poor nutrition during development
  • Unknown cause in up to 50% of cases (idiopathic)
Prevention & Lifestyle
  • Take anti-epileptic medications exactly as prescribed, never skip doses
  • Get adequate sleep, sleep deprivation is a major seizure trigger
  • Avoid alcohol and recreational drugs
  • Manage stress through mindfulness and relaxation techniques
  • Avoid known personal triggers (flashing lights, fever)
  • Wear medical ID bracelet so others can help during a seizure
Treatment
  • Anti-epileptic drugs (AEDs), Levetiracetam, Valproate, Carbamazepine, Lamotrigine
  • 70% of patients become seizure-free with appropriate medication
  • Surgery for drug-resistant focal epilepsy, resect the seizure focus
  • Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) device for refractory cases
  • Ketogenic diet, high-fat, low-carb diet proven effective in children
  • SUDEP prevention, never stop medication suddenly
