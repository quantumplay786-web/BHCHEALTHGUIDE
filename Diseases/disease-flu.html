Influenza (Flu)

A highly contagious respiratory illness caused by Influenza A, B, or C viruses that infects the nose, throat, and lungs.

Symptoms

Flu symptoms come on suddenly and are more severe than the common cold. They typically appear 1, 4 days after exposure and last 5, 7 days.

  • Sudden high fever (38, 41°C) with chills
  • Severe muscle aches and body pains
  • Persistent dry cough and sore throat
  • Extreme fatigue and weakness
  • Headache, runny or stuffy nose
  • Vomiting and diarrhea (more common in children)
Causes

Influenza viruses are RNA viruses belonging to the Orthomyxoviridae family. They mutate rapidly, which is why a new flu vaccine is needed every year.

  • Influenza A, most common, causes seasonal epidemics and pandemics
  • Influenza B, seasonal outbreaks, less severe than A
  • Influenza C, mild illness, not responsible for epidemics
  • H1N1, H3N2, common circulating subtypes of Influenza A
Prevention

Annual flu vaccination is the single most effective way to prevent influenza. The vaccine is updated each year to match circulating strains.

  • Get the annual flu vaccine every autumn
  • Wash hands frequently with soap and water
  • Avoid close contact with sick people
  • Cover mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing
  • Stay home when sick to prevent spreading
  • Disinfect commonly touched surfaces
Treatment

Most flu cases resolve on their own with rest and fluids. Antiviral medications can reduce severity if started within 48 hours of symptoms.

  • Rest and drink plenty of fluids
  • Oseltamivir (Tamiflu), antiviral for high risk patients
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen for fever and pain
  • Avoid aspirin in children (risk of Reye's syndrome)
  • Seek medical care if symptoms worsen after day 5
  • Hospitalization for pneumonia complications
