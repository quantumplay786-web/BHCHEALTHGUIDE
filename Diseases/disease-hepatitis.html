Hepatitis B

A serious liver infection caused by the Hepatitis B virus (HBV) that can become chronic and lead to liver failure, cirrhosis, or liver cancer.

Symptoms

Many people with hepatitis B do not have symptoms when first infected. Some develop a short term acute illness. Others develop a long term chronic infection that can cause serious liver damage over decades.

  • Jaundice, yellowing of skin and eyes
  • Dark urine and pale stools
  • Extreme fatigue and weakness
  • Nausea, vomiting, and loss of appetite
  • Abdominal pain in the right upper area
  • Fever and joint pain (acute phase)
Causes

Hepatitis B is caused by the hepatitis B virus (HBV), a partially double-stranded DNA virus of the Hepadnaviridae family. It attacks liver cells and can persist for decades.

  • Hepatitis B virus (HBV), highly infectious, 50, 100x more contagious than HIV
  • Transmitted via infected blood, sexual fluids, or from mother to child at birth
  • Sharing needles, syringes, or drug equipment
  • Unprotected sexual intercourse with infected person
Prevention
  • Hepatitis B vaccine, safe, effective, 3-dose series
  • Use condoms during sexual intercourse
  • Never share needles, syringes, or personal items
  • Ensure sterile medical and tattoo equipment
  • Test during pregnancy, treat if positive to protect baby
  • HBIG (Hepatitis B immune globulin) after high risk exposure
Treatment
  • Acute hepatitis B, rest, fluids, and symptom management
  • Antiviral medications: Tenofovir, Entecavir for chronic HBV
  • Interferon injections for some chronic cases
  • Regular liver function tests and viral load monitoring
  • Avoid alcohol, severely worsens liver damage
  • Liver transplant considered in end-stage liver disease
