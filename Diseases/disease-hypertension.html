Hypertension

High blood pressure, often called the silent killer because it has no obvious symptoms but significantly increases the risk of heart attack, stroke, and kidney failure.

Symptoms

Hypertension is one of the most common chronic conditions worldwide, affecting 1 in 3 adults. Most people do not know they have it because it typically causes no symptoms until serious damage has occurred.

  • Usually no symptoms, known as the silent killer
  • Severe headaches (hypertensive crisis)
  • Dizziness and nosebleeds in very high BP
  • Shortness of breath and chest pain
  • Visual changes and blurred vision
  • Blood in urine (sign of kidney damage)
Causes

Blood pressure is consistently above 130/80 mmHg. Primary hypertension develops gradually over years; secondary hypertension has a specific underlying cause.

  • Primary hypertension, genetic factors, age, lifestyle, obesity
  • Secondary, kidney disease, thyroid disorders, sleep apnea
  • High salt diet, physical inactivity, excessive alcohol
  • Chronic stress, smoking, and certain medications
Prevention
  • Reduce salt intake to less than 5g per day
  • Follow DASH diet, rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains
  • Exercise 30 minutes daily, brisk walking lowers BP
  • Maintain healthy weight, BMI 18.5, 24.9
  • Limit alcohol to 1-2 units per day maximum
  • Stop smoking and manage stress through relaxation techniques
Treatment
  • ACE inhibitors (e.g. Lisinopril), relax blood vessels
  • ARBs (e.g. Losartan), block angiotensin, lower BP
  • Calcium channel blockers (e.g. Amlodipine), widen arteries
  • Diuretics (water pills), reduce fluid volume
  • Beta-blockers for specific cases
  • Regular monitoring with home blood pressure device
