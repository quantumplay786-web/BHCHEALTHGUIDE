Migraine

A complex neurological condition causing recurrent episodes of severe, often one-sided headache with associated symptoms like nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light and sound.

Symptoms

Migraine affects approximately 1 billion people worldwide, making it the third most prevalent illness globally. It is more common in women (3:1 ratio) and most frequently occurs between ages 25-55.

  • Severe, throbbing or pulsating headache, often one-sided
  • Aura, visual disturbances (zigzag lines, blind spots) in 25-30% of cases
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Extreme sensitivity to light (photophobia) and sound (phonophobia)
  • Prodrome, mood changes, food cravings, yawning hours before headache
  • Postdrome, fatigue, cognitive fog ('migraine hangover') after attack
Causes

Migraines involve complex changes in brain chemistry and nerve pathways. The trigeminal nerve system and serotonin play major roles.

  • Cortical spreading depression, a wave of electrical activity across the brain
  • Trigeminal nerve activation releasing inflammatory peptides (CGRP)
  • Genetic predisposition, 70% of migraineurs have a family history
  • Hormonal changes, especially around menstruation in women
  • Common triggers: stress, sleep changes, skipping meals, bright lights, alcohol
  • Weather changes and strong smells can also trigger attacks
Prevention
  • Keep a migraine diary to identify personal triggers
  • Maintain regular sleep schedule, even on weekends
  • Stay well hydrated and do not skip meals
  • Manage stress with mindfulness, yoga, or therapy
  • Limit caffeine intake and avoid known dietary triggers
  • Wear sunglasses in bright light and use blue light filters on screens
Treatment
  • Acute (abort attacks): Triptans (Sumatriptan), NSAIDs, anti-nausea medications
  • CGRP antagonists (Ubrogepant, Rimegepant), newer fast-acting options
  • Preventive (reduce frequency): Beta-blockers, Topiramate, Amitriptyline
  • CGRP monoclonal antibodies (Aimovig, Ajovy), monthly/quarterly injections
  • Botulinum toxin injections for chronic migraine (15+ days/month)
  • Biofeedback and cognitive behavioral therapy help reduce frequency
