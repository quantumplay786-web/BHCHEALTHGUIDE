Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

A mental health disorder where people experience recurring unwanted thoughts (obsessions) and engage in repetitive behaviors (compulsions) in an attempt to reduce anxiety.

Symptoms

OCD affects approximately 2.5% of the global population. It is not about liking things clean or organized, it causes significant distress and can consume hours of a person's day. It is highly treatable with the right therapy.

  • Obsessions, persistent, intrusive, unwanted thoughts or images
  • Compulsions, repetitive behaviors or mental acts to reduce anxiety
  • Common themes: contamination and cleaning, symmetry and ordering, harm and checking
  • Time-consuming rituals that interfere with daily life
  • Significant distress and insight that obsessions are irrational
  • Avoidance of situations that trigger obsessional thoughts
Causes

OCD results from a combination of genetic, neurobiological, and environmental factors involving dysregulation of serotonin and circuits in the brain's frontal lobe.

  • Abnormal activity in the orbitofrontal cortex-striatum-thalamus circuit
  • Serotonin dysregulation, SSRIs are effective, confirming serotonin involvement
  • Genetic factors, 40-65% heritability; family history increases risk
  • Stressful or traumatic life events can trigger onset
  • PANDAS (pediatric autoimmune), OCD triggered by strep infection in children
  • Personality traits, perfectionism, high responsibility, threat overestimation
Prevention
  • Early therapy reduces the chance of symptoms becoming severe and chronic
  • Avoid reassurance-seeking behaviors, they maintain the OCD cycle
  • Do not accommodate OCD rituals, gradual exposure is more effective
  • Reduce overall stress through exercise and mindfulness
  • Build a support network who understands OCD
  • Online CBT programs available if specialist access is limited
Treatment
  • Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP), gold standard therapy; 60-80% effective
  • SSRIs at higher doses, Fluoxetine, Sertraline, Fluvoxamine
  • Clomipramine (tricyclic) for treatment-resistant OCD
  • Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) as complement to ERP
  • Deep Brain Stimulation for severe, treatment-resistant OCD
  • BetterHelp, online therapists specializing in OCD and ERP
Quick Facts
Key Distinction:
OCD is ego-dystonic, the obsessions are unwanted and distressing
Recovery:
70%+ respond to ERP therapy. Full recovery is possible.
