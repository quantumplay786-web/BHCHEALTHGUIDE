Parkinson's Disease

A progressive neurodegenerative disorder that mainly affects movement, caused by the loss of dopamine-producing nerve cells in the brain's substantia nigra region.

Symptoms

Parkinson's affects approximately 10 million people worldwide. While there is currently no cure, treatments significantly improve quality of life. It is the second most common neurodegenerative disorder after Alzheimer's disease.

  • Resting tremor, shaking in hands, arms, or jaw at rest
  • Bradykinesia, slowness of movement, reduced arm swing
  • Muscle rigidity, stiffness in limbs and trunk
  • Postural instability, impaired balance and coordination
  • Micrographia, small, cramped handwriting
  • Soft speech, masked facial expression, shuffling walk
Causes & Risk Factors

Parkinson's results from the death of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra. The cause is a combination of genetic and environmental factors.

  • Loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra
  • Accumulation of Lewy bodies (alpha-synuclein protein) in brain cells
  • Genetic mutations, LRRK2, SNCA, PRKN genes in familial cases
  • Environmental exposures, pesticides, herbicides, heavy metals
  • Age is the greatest risk factor, most cases after age 60
  • Males are 1.5x more likely to develop Parkinson's
Prevention & Lifestyle
  • Regular aerobic exercise, shown to slow disease progression
  • Avoid pesticide exposure where possible
  • Mediterranean diet, associated with lower Parkinson's risk
  • Moderate coffee consumption linked to lower risk in studies
  • Stay mentally and socially active
  • Physical therapy and boxing programs help maintain mobility
Treatment
  • Levodopa/Carbidopa, gold standard, replenishes dopamine
  • Dopamine agonists, Pramipexole, Ropinirole for early disease
  • MAO-B inhibitors, Selegiline, Rasagiline to slow dopamine breakdown
  • Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS), surgical option for advanced cases
  • Physiotherapy, speech therapy, and occupational therapy
  • Amantadine for dyskinesia (involuntary movements from long term levodopa)
