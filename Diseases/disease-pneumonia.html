Pneumonia

An infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs, which may fill with fluid or pus. It can range from mild to serious and potentially fatal, especially in the elderly, infants, and immunocompromised individuals.

Symptoms

Pneumonia is the leading infectious cause of death worldwide, killing over 2.5 million people annually. Bacterial pneumonia is the most common form in adults.

  • Chest pain that worsens when breathing or coughing
  • Cough producing phlegm (yellow, green, or bloody)
  • Fever, sweating, and chills
  • Shortness of breath even with normal activities
  • Fatigue and loss of appetite
  • Confusion or altered mental status in elderly
Causes

Pneumonia can be caused by bacteria, viruses, or fungi. The cause determines the appropriate treatment.

  • Streptococcus pneumoniae, most common bacterial cause in adults
  • Influenza virus and SARS-CoV-2, common viral causes
  • Mycoplasma pneumoniae, causes walking pneumonia
  • Pneumocystis jirovecii, fungal pneumonia in immunocompromised
Prevention
  • Pneumococcal vaccine (PCV13, PPSV23) for high risk groups
  • Annual influenza vaccine reduces pneumonia risk
  • COVID-19 vaccination
  • Wash hands frequently and avoid touching face
  • Do not smoke, smoking damages lung defenses
  • Adequate nutrition and rest to maintain immune function
Treatment
  • Antibiotics for bacterial pneumonia, Amoxicillin, Azithromycin
  • Antivirals for viral pneumonia (e.g. Oseltamivir for flu-related)
  • Oxygen therapy for low blood oxygen levels
  • Rest and drink plenty of fluids
  • Hospitalization for severe cases or high risk patients
  • ICU care with ventilator support for critical pneumonia
