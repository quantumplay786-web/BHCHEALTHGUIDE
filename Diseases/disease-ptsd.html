PTSD (Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder)

A mental health condition triggered by experiencing or witnessing a traumatic event, causing lasting psychological distress, flashbacks, nightmares, and altered emotional responses.

Symptoms

PTSD affects approximately 3.9% of the global population. It can develop after any traumatic event, not just combat or major disasters. Women are twice as likely as men to develop PTSD after a trauma.

  • Intrusive memories, flashbacks, nightmares of the traumatic event
  • Hyperarousal, being easily startled, feeling tense, difficulty sleeping
  • Avoidance, staying away from places, people, or thoughts linked to the trauma
  • Negative changes in thinking and mood, guilt, shame, emotional numbness
  • Dissociation, feeling detached from oneself or surroundings
  • Physical reactions to trauma reminders, rapid heart rate, sweating
Causes

PTSD develops when the normal processing of trauma is disrupted, causing the memory to be stored in a fragmented, highly emotional state that re-activates when triggered.

  • Traumatic experiences, combat, assault, accidents, natural disasters, abuse
  • Dysregulation of the amygdala (fear response) and hippocampus (memory)
  • Low cortisol levels after trauma, different from normal stress response
  • Pre-existing anxiety, depression, or lack of social support increases risk
  • Childhood trauma, adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) increase vulnerability
  • Severity, duration, and proximity to the trauma affect likelihood of PTSD
Prevention
  • Seek support quickly after trauma, social support is highly protective
  • Psychological first aid within days of trauma reduces PTSD risk
  • Avoid alcohol and drugs as coping, worsen long term outcomes
  • Maintain routine and structure in daily life
  • Process the event through talking with trusted people or a therapist
  • Early therapy (Trauma-Focused CBT) prevents PTSD from becoming chronic
Treatment
  • Trauma-Focused CBT (TF-CBT), processing trauma memories safely
  • EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing), highly effective
  • Prolonged Exposure (PE) therapy, facing trauma triggers in safe context
  • SSRIs, Sertraline, Paroxetine (FDA-approved for PTSD)
  • Prazosin for trauma-related nightmares
  • Group therapy and peer support programs for veterans and survivors
