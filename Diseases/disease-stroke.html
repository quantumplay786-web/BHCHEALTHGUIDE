Stroke (Brain Attack)

A medical emergency where blood supply to part of the brain is cut off, causing brain cells to die. Every minute without treatment, 1.9 million neurons are lost, time is brain.

Symptoms

Stroke is the second leading cause of death and the third leading cause of disability worldwide. Acting within 3-4.5 hours of symptom onset dramatically improves survival and recovery outcomes.

  • Face drooping, one side of the face droops or is numb
  • Arm weakness, one arm is weak or numb when raised
  • Speech difficulty, slurred speech, confusion, unable to speak
  • Sudden severe headache with no known cause
  • Sudden vision loss in one or both eyes
  • Sudden trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance
Causes

Strokes occur in two main forms: ischemic (blockage of blood vessel) accounting for 87% of cases, and hemorrhagic (rupture of a blood vessel).

  • Ischemic stroke, blood clot blocking an artery supplying the brain
  • Hemorrhagic stroke, blood vessel ruptures and bleeds into the brain
  • High blood pressure, the single biggest risk factor for stroke
  • Atrial fibrillation, irregular heartbeat increases clot formation risk
  • Diabetes, high cholesterol, smoking, obesity, physical inactivity
  • TIA (transient ischemic attack), mini-stroke warning sign
Prevention
  • Control blood pressure, most important stroke prevention measure
  • Do not smoke, smoking doubles stroke risk
  • Treat atrial fibrillation with anticoagulants as prescribed
  • Control diabetes and maintain healthy cholesterol levels
  • Exercise 150 minutes per week; maintain healthy weight
  • Limit alcohol and eat a heart-healthy diet
Treatment
  • Call emergency services immediately, do not drive yourself
  • tPA (clot-busting drug), must be given within 3-4.5 hours of ischemic stroke
  • Mechanical thrombectomy, catheter to physically remove clot (up to 24 hours)
  • Surgery for hemorrhagic stroke to relieve pressure
  • Aspirin started within 48 hours of ischemic stroke
  • Rehabilitation, physiotherapy, speech therapy, occupational therapy
Quick Facts
Remember FAST:
Face, Arms, Speech, Time, call emergency if any sign appears
Recovery:
30% make full recovery; early rehab dramatically improves outcomes
