Tuberculosis (TB)

A potentially serious infectious disease that mainly affects the lungs, caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacteria. It spreads through the air when infected people cough, sneeze, or speak.

Symptoms

TB comes in two forms: latent TB (infection with no symptoms, not contagious) and active TB disease (symptoms present, contagious). Active TB must be treated promptly.

  • Persistent cough lasting 3 or more weeks
  • Coughing up blood or mucus
  • Chest pain when breathing or coughing
  • Unintentional weight loss
  • Fatigue and night sweats
  • Fever and chills
Causes

TB is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, a slow-growing bacterium that can lay dormant in the body for years before becoming active.

  • Mycobacterium tuberculosis, gram-positive, acid-fast bacillus
  • Airborne transmission, spreads when infected person coughs or sneezes
  • Risk increases with prolonged close contact with active TB patient
  • HIV co-infection dramatically increases TB risk
Prevention
  • BCG vaccination, recommended for infants in high-TB-burden countries
  • Complete the full course of TB treatment, prevents drug-resistant TB
  • Good ventilation in living and working spaces
  • Test and treat latent TB in high risk individuals
  • Wear N95 mask when caring for active TB patients
  • Healthcare workers should get regular TB screening
Treatment
  • Standard 6-month DOTS regimen: Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol
  • Never skip doses, leads to drug-resistant TB (MDR-TB)
  • Drug-resistant TB requires 18, 24 months of treatment
  • Directly Observed Therapy (DOTS) ensures treatment completion
  • Nutritional support improves outcomes
  • Isolation during first 2 weeks of active treatment
Quick Facts
Latent vs Active:
~10% of latent TB becomes active disease
Drug Resistance:
MDR-TB is a growing global health threat
