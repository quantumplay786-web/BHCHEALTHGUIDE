Typhoid Fever

A serious and potentially fatal bacterial infection caused by Salmonella typhi, spread through contaminated food and water. Particularly common in areas with poor sanitation.

Symptoms

Typhoid fever remains a serious public health problem in many parts of Asia, Africa, and Latin America. Early antibiotic treatment is essential to prevent complications.

  • Gradually rising fever reaching 40°C (104°F)
  • Weakness and fatigue
  • Stomach pain, headache, and loss of appetite
  • Diarrhea or constipation
  • Rose-colored spots on chest and abdomen
  • Enlarged spleen and liver
Causes

Typhoid fever is caused by the bacterium Salmonella enterica serovar Typhi (S. typhi). It is exclusively a human pathogen.

  • Salmonella typhi bacterium in contaminated water or food
  • Spread by poor hand hygiene, fecal-oral route
  • Contaminated drinking water from sewage
  • Raw fruits, vegetables washed in contaminated water
Prevention
  • Typhoid vaccine, oral and injectable options available
  • Drink only bottled or boiled water
  • Eat food that is thoroughly cooked and still hot
  • Wash hands with soap before eating and after toilet use
  • Avoid raw vegetables and salads in high risk areas
  • Proper sanitation and sewage treatment
Treatment
  • Antibiotics: Ciprofloxacin, Azithromycin, or Ceftriaxone
  • Dexamethasone for severe cases with neurological complications
  • Rest and stay well hydrated
  • Oral rehydration for diarrhea management
  • Surgery may be needed for intestinal perforation
  • Carriers should be treated to prevent ongoing spread
Quick Facts
Endemic Regions:
South Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, South-East Asia

Intestinal perforation, encephalopathy (5-10% if untreated)
